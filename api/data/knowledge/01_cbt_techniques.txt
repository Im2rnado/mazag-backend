# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) Techniques for Mental Health

## What is CBT?
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is one of the most evidence-based psychological treatments. It's based on the idea that our thoughts, feelings, and behaviors are all connected — and that changing unhelpful thought patterns can improve how we feel and act.

## Core Principle: The CBT Triangle
- **Thoughts** → shape how we feel
- **Feelings** → influence our behavior
- **Behaviors** → reinforce our thoughts

Breaking any one side of this triangle can break a negative cycle.

## Common Cognitive Distortions

**All-or-Nothing Thinking**: "If I'm not perfect, I'm a total failure."
Challenge: Look for the middle ground. Most situations exist on a spectrum.

**Catastrophizing**: "This headache means I have a brain tumor."
Challenge: Ask what the most likely explanation is. Rate the actual probability.

**Mind Reading**: "She didn't reply because she hates me."
Challenge: Are there other explanations? Have you checked with the person?

**Fortune Telling**: "I know the presentation will go badly."
Challenge: Have you predicted badly before? What evidence do you have?

**Overgeneralization**: "I always fail at relationships."
Challenge: Always? Never? Find one exception.

**Personalization**: "The team failed because of me."
Challenge: What were the other contributing factors?

**Emotional Reasoning**: "I feel stupid, therefore I am stupid."
Challenge: Feelings are not facts. What would you say to a friend who felt this way?

**Should Statements**: "I should be able to handle this."
Challenge: Replace 'should' with 'it would be nice if.' Remove self-punishment.

**Disqualifying the Positive**: "They only complimented me to be nice."
Challenge: Why is the negative interpretation more true than the positive one?

## Practical CBT Techniques

### Thought Record
1. Write down the situation
2. Identify the automatic thought (the first thing that came to mind)
3. Rate belief in that thought (0–100%)
4. List evidence FOR the thought
5. List evidence AGAINST the thought
6. Write a more balanced, alternative thought
7. Re-rate emotion and belief in original thought

### Behavioral Activation
When depressed, we often withdraw from activities that bring joy. This worsens depression.
- Schedule one small, pleasant activity per day
- Track your mood before and after
- Gradually increase activities over time
- The action comes BEFORE motivation, not after

### Behavioral Experiment
- Identify a feared prediction ("If I speak up, everyone will laugh")
- Design a small experiment to test it
- Carry out the experiment
- Record what actually happened
- Draw a conclusion: was your prediction accurate?

### Socratic Questioning
A guided questioning technique to examine the evidence for and against a belief:
- "What is the evidence for this thought?"
- "What would you say to a friend in the same situation?"
- "What is the realistic worst-case scenario? Could you handle that?"
- "What is the most helpful way of thinking about this?"

### Graded Exposure
For anxiety and avoidance: create a fear ladder from least scary to most scary, then face each step.
- Start with the easiest step
- Stay in the situation until anxiety reduces on its own (don't escape)
- Repeat until that step feels manageable, then move up

## When CBT Helps Most
- Depression and low mood
- Anxiety disorders (GAD, social anxiety, OCD, phobias)
- Panic disorder
- PTSD
- Low self-esteem
- Anger management
- Eating disorders
- Insomnia
